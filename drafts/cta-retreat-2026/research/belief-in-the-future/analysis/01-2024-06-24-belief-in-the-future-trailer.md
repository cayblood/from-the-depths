## Summary

The host opens by naming the feeling that the world is moving too fast (AI, climate, brain-computer interfaces, surveillance) and that most people carry these concerns as "a weird mush of excitement and deep concern." His particular complaint is the sense of inevitability: technology talks and is talked about as if on autopilot, so asking whether its products are good or bad seems pointless, which leaves people "lousy and disempowered." He proposes religion as an unexplored source of ideas for navigating this, because religion is where billions of people "develop their ideas about right and wrong," practice community, and organize around values. He concedes the relationship between religion and tech is "somewhere between strained and non-existent," that tech leaders dismiss religious perspectives as uninformed, that religious leaders often agree, and that bad blood over abortion and stem cells makes many want to keep the two apart. He promises stories about communities that negotiated their own relationship to technology, ancient robots, space travel as religion, and the Pope's deepfakes, and pitches the show to anyone, religious or not.
