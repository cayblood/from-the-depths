# 19. A Catholic and a Jew read the Pope's AI encyclical together (2026-05-29)

## Relevance

Medium. The episode is mostly about the genre and reception of *Magnifica Humanitas*, but it gives Carl direct material on the "quasi-religious AI world" (Chris Olah on stage with the Pope), a Catholic account of formation as a trajectory with productive suffering, and the encyclical's explicit condemnation of transhumanism, which a Christian Transhumanist audience will have heard about.

## Who

- Host: DZ Kalman (Speaker 1; rendered "Deezy Kallman" in the transcript).
- Guest: Brian Green (Speaker 2), Director of Technology Ethics at the Markkula Center for Applied Ethics, Santa Clara University; teaches AI ethics and space ethics at Santa Clara's Graduate School of Engineering. Identified by the host in the intro.

## Summary

A rush-produced special on Pope Leo XIV's first encyclical, *Magnifica Humanitas* (on AI, signed May 15, 2026, about 42,000 words, produced in consultation with the tech sector; Anthropic co-founder Chris Olah spoke at its debut). Green explains the genre: an encyclical addressed to "all people of goodwill," signed on the anniversary of *Rerum Novarum* (1891), that works by subsidiarity, proposing ideas from on high and expecting free agents at the local level to act on them. The two read the document's central Babel-versus-Nehemiah metaphor (monolithic homogenizing project versus a wall rebuilt family by family, each owning a section), its careful refusal to equate machine "intelligence" with human intelligence while also refusing to deny future moral status, its condemnation of transhumanism as anti-human because it treats limitation as a bug, its treatment of the attention economy as addiction that strips agency, and its explicit embrace of the development of doctrine. Kalman raises Jewish discomfort with Jerusalem as a universal symbol, worries that length is a defense against being read, and notes the shift from "church and state" to "church and firm" symbolized by Olah's presence.

## Bears on

- Thread 7 (quasi-religious AI world): the clearest material here. Anthropic was the most prominent tech consultant on the encyclical; Olah sat "two or three chairs" from the Pope and asked for tough outside critics. Kalman frames this as a new church-and-firm relationship replacing church-and-state.
- Thread 1 (formation versus rules): Green's account of Catholic ethics as the problem of moving from abstract rule ("do good and avoid evil") to concrete life, with subsidiarity as the mechanism; and Christianity's picture of persons as "on a trajectory from here to there," formed through productive difficulty. Also the encyclical's point that the Pope cannot command, he must persuade free agents (compare D&C 121).
- Thread 4 (costly signaling, trial): the distinction between productive and destructive suffering; limitations as a feature, not a bug.
- Thread 6 (personhood): the Church declines to rule out AI moral status (angels, animals, extraterrestrials; the 1277 condemnations), while insisting AI is not human.
- Thread 3 (hermeneutic of suspicion), weakly: Green's contrast between "the mob shouting you're doing everything wrong" and talking to a person and asking for a specific change.

## Tension or objection

- The encyclical, as Green reads it, condemns transhumanism outright as anti-human, treating limitation as constitutive of humanity. Carl is MTA president speaking to Christian Transhumanists; he should expect the room to know this document and should either distinguish his project from what the encyclical attacks (instrumentalizing human nature) or address it head on. Green's own healer/teacher framing (41:12) offers an opening: the Church accepts correcting defects, so the dispute is about which limitations are productive, not whether to transcend any.
- Kalman's point (24:22) that "we're all building this together" rhetoric can be a fantasy covering a narrower project applies to any talk that presents religion or AI labs as holding an image of the good "in common." Who is actually in the room when the image is set?
- Green's note that the Church was "very slow on the uptake" on slavery (49:48) cuts against treating religious traditions as reliably good alignment labs; the lab produced bad results for centuries before repairing them. Carl can use this as evidence of repair, but a skeptic will use it the other way.
- The document declines to define AI's nature and refuses to grant moral status now; Green's "it's an abstraction of human nature" (33:55) sits uneasily with Carl's "be slow to delete" if the audience takes the Catholic line.
