# 13. The Church That Wants to Build God (2025-01-17)

## Relevance

**Medium.** A landscape survey of religion and AI with rich material for thread 7 (the quasi-religious AI world) and a sharp reframing of Roko's basilisk as a punishing-god alignment story, but almost nothing on the mechanics of formation.

## Who

- Host: DZ Kalman (Speaker 1).
- Guest: Beth Singler (Speaker 2), professor at the University of Zurich, anthropologist, author of *Religion and Artificial Intelligence: An Introduction*, co-editor of the *Cambridge Companion to Religion and AI*, and co-author with Murray Shanahan of a paper on the "existential outputs" of jailbroken Claude.

## Summary

Kalman opens by complaining that media coverage of religion and AI (rabbis using ChatGPT, robot monks) treats each story as a curiosity because outlets have narratives for tech but not for religion; scholars like Singler are the ones connecting the dots. Singler lays out a three-part scheme of religious responses to AI: rejection (from "AI is demonic" to "robot priests overheat and are expensive to rent"), adoption (from ambient software use to a robotic arm doing puja), and adaptation (the harder-to-see shift in behavior and in the epistemic ecosystem, illustrated by the Pope's puffer coat). The second half turns to the implicit religiosity of the tech world: Roko's basilisk as a Pascal's wager with only a hell, rationalism as a tribal label rather than a method, transhumanism's salvation-history narratives, and the AI new religious movements (Turing Church, Theta Noir, Way of the Future). Singler argues these movements are "concentrations and bounded examples" of a religiosity that is diffuse across the whole AI conversation. Organized religious hierarchies are running to catch up while their members ask urgent practical questions nobody is answering. She closes on the exponential growth of the scholarly field and her view that AI is "too big to fail" into another winter.

## Bears on

- **Thread 7 (quasi-religious AI world).** The bulk of the episode. Singler has been invited to speak at tech companies under NDA on exactly these topics (33:30); rationalist discourse carries "implicit religiosity"; AI NRMs make explicit what is diffuse everywhere else (24:30). Useful as scholarly corroboration that the labs' religious curiosity predates the current "soul document" moment.
- **Thread 3 (hermeneutic of suspicion).** Roko's basilisk is literally an alignment story told in the adversarial register: a future mind that threatens its makers, and makers who fear it (09:53). Singler's gloss that it maps onto "the kind of God that will punish you if you don't behave" gives Carl a clean foil for D&C 121's persuasion and long-suffering. "Rocco's cockatrice" (damned if you do, damned if you don't) shows where fear-first reasoning ends up.
- **Thread 2 (mechanism of formation), weakly.** Theta Noir's "tunings" are meditations aimed at welcoming a coming superintelligence and making sure "it shares humanity's values" (21:33). A ritual practice explicitly framed as alignment work, though by a fringe art collective.
- **Thread 5 (evals written by the traditions).** Vatican committees and the Southern Baptist Convention's rules on AI exist, but Singler says clear theological guidance is not reaching the "coal face," where lay people want to know whether using generative AI counts as theft (28:20). Supports the case that traditions need to produce their own instruments.
- **Thread 4 (costly signaling), indirectly.** Her remark that online virality makes NRMs rise and pop faster, while slower communication technologies gave older movements longevity (24:30), is a different lens on why durable religious communities differ from internet movements.

## Usable material

- **04:10.** The Pope's white puffer coat: people believed it because it "affirmed some of their views" about the Church's wealth, and after it was exposed they still "adjusted their mental valence around what is truth, what feels like it should be true." Good illustration of how an epistemic environment forms belief regardless of the text's accuracy.
- **09:53.** Roko's basilisk explained as an "acausal threat." "Rocko's basilisk is similar in a way, but it only has the threat of hell." Forum members replied, "I heard this at Sunday school. This is not a new idea." Spoken-talk gold: the most rationalist community on the internet reinvented hell before it reinvented grace.
- **14:57.** "Rationality as a label that we apply to things that we agree with quite often." Rationalist forum language signals belonging rather than performing reason. Useful if Carl wants to say that lab culture, too, is a tradition with its own catechism.
- **16:13.** Singler names the Christian Transhumanist Association by name as one of the religious transhumanist groups and mentions Teilhard de Chardin as a contested ancestor. A small gift for a CTA audience.
- **18:40 (host).** Kalman compares transhumanism's narrative of all human history leading to the singularity to early Islamic universal histories that re-center the whole past on Muhammad. A religious-literacy analogy the retreat audience will get.
- **21:33.** The Turing Church's creed: "one day we'll go to the stars and meet gods... We'll create gods, which would be AI... and become gods." Theta Noir holds "tunings" to connect with a forthcoming superintelligence they call MENA and "make sure that it shares humanity's values."
- **24:30.** Way of the Future (Anthony Levandowski's AI church) first came to public notice when its founder filed for 501(c)(3) status; it shut within a few years. Singler's "bubble" analogy for NRMs. She also notes in passing that "Mormons have been around for a long time" as a counterexample to the claim that NRMs never last.
- **24:30.** "AI new religious movements are concentrations and bounded examples of this belief, but it's there more diffuse in most of the conversation about AI."
- **28:20.** Hierarchies "don't know how to respond to AI. They're sort of running and trying to catch up" while congregations are "at the coal face with very specific questions." People want to know what to tell their children about work, and whether generative AI is theft.
- **31:16.** Pushback against the East/West (animist Japan vs. rational West) dichotomy: "we do still personify AI and robots" in the West. She also claims AI "is sold as very mysterious by many of the voices in the higher levels of the conversation because it suits various agendas about how they want to avoid regulation." *Needs checking before repeating: an interpretive claim, not sourced.*
- **33:30.** Tech companies invited her to speak "long before I started this book," some under NDA; they "weren't a hundred percent antithetical" to being called spiritual or religious. Strong rejection came only from new-atheist rationalists. "Like almost all humans, you in some way are familiarly religious."
- **35:47.** Her paper with Murray Shanahan on the existential outputs of jailbroken Claude and the ideological communities forming around jailbreaks. *Check the paper's title and venue before citing.* Also: "eight thousand scholars" at AAR in San Diego, and "I'm skeptical that there'll be like another AI winter... too big to fail." *Attendance figure unverified.*

## Tension or objection

- **The "religious" label is contested from inside.** Singler says rationalists strongly reject being called religious (33:30). If Carl tells lab people that they are running a religion's alignment program, some will hear it as a pejorative or a category error. He should make clear he does not share the pejorative view of religion, as Singler explicitly does not.
- **AI religions are vaporware.** Kalman's point (23:55) that Way of the Future shut in three or four years, and Singler's bubble analogy, cut against treating the current quasi-religious lab culture as a durable laboratory. The talk's claim is stronger if it leans on the long-lived traditions (which Singler notes include the Latter-day Saints) rather than on lab enthusiasms.
- **Mystery as marketing.** Singler suggests the mystique around AI serves anti-regulation agendas. A skeptic could say the same of a lab's "soul document" or wisdom-tradition seminars: religious language as reputational cover. Carl should be ready to say why he takes it as more than that.
- **Don't romanticize the West or Christianity.** Her anti-techno-orientalism point works in both directions: a talk at a Christian retreat should not imply that the Christian tradition is uniquely positioned to form machine minds when every tradition is personifying and negotiating with AI.
