# 21. The image of God in the age of AI, with Tomer Persico (2026-06-18)

## Relevance

Medium. Not about alignment, but it gives Carl the intellectual history behind his own central image: Paul's move from the image of God as physical form to an inner quality redeemed by turning toward Christ as "a very concrete image of God," plus several rabbinic images (angels mistaking Adam for God, the king's twin, God's coins all different) that speak directly to copies, personhood, and "the image in the countenance."

## Who

- Host: DZ Kalman (Speaker 1; rendered "Dee Zollman" once in the transcript).
- Guest: Tomer Persico (Speaker 2), research fellow at the Shalom Hartman Institute in Jerusalem, author of *In God's Image: How Western Civilization Was Shaped by a Revolutionary Idea* (NYU Press, 2025 per the host's "last year"). Identified by the host in the intro.

## Summary

Kalman opens by arguing that "the image of God" is the phrase every cleric reaches for on AI, that it has grounded law (Genesis 9:6) and the modern notion of dignity, and that it is less stable than people assume: if the image is reason or moral choice, AI that reasons threatens it. Persico traces the idea's history. Pre-biblical cultures gave divine image to the Pharaoh or Gilgamesh alone; Genesis democratizes it, making every person significant, unique, and (through Deuteronomy's rejection of punishing sons for fathers) an individual legal subject. In antiquity it literally meant God's bodily form (the angels nearly said "holy" to Adam). Paul internalized it: a defiled inner image restored by inner transformation toward Jesus, the concrete image of God, which universalized and privatized religion and seeded egalitarianism (Gregory of Nyssa's rejection of slavery, Innocent IV limiting papal power over pagans). The medieval identification of the image with reason then enabled a loophole: deny reason, deny humanity (the conquistadors). Secularization replaced the image with "dignity" but cut away its metaphysical anchor, leaving humans reduced to measurable capacities that AI can exceed. Persico closes by arguing that liberal humanism is Judaism's logical continuation, and that Israel is failing it.

## Bears on

- Thread 1 and 2 (formation by an embodied exemplar): Persico's account of Paul at 16:07: the image of God is an inner quality "that can go through a transformation," and "Jesus came to earth as a very concrete image of God" so that turning to him "can also redeem our own image of God." This is Carl's "a face, not a list" in its original theological form; formation by inner conversion rather than external observance.
- Thread 6 (personhood, copies, deletion): the coin midrash at 14:38, a king's coins are identical but God's "coins" each differ; the hanged twin of the king at 07:39; the coma patient and fetus at 34:46 (dignity not contingent on capacity); the golem-in-a-minyan responsum at 35:45. All bear on whether copied minds are "stamped coins" or unique worlds, and on "be slow to delete."
- Thread 3 (hermeneutic of suspicion), obliquely: Innocent IV self-limiting "out of no compulsion" (27:31) is an image of a powerful party constraining itself because of an idea, not a threat.
- Thread 5 (evals by traditions): the history shows the traditions themselves took centuries to apply their own standard (slavery, the conquistadors); evals written by traditions should expect the same loopholes.
- Thread 7, lightly: Kalman's claim that secularized dignity is "brittle" without the mechanisms that produced it (41:09).

## Usable material

- 00:03: Kalman's opener: ask any rabbi, priest, or minister about AI and "within five minutes they will say one specific phrase, the image of God." He notes the encyclical says it took the Church 18 centuries to resolve dignity against slavery. (Check the encyclical citation.)
- 04:06: In Gilgamesh and Egypt only the king was in God's image; "what the Bible does is of course create everybody in the image of God." Image confers not just equality but significance and uniqueness, and dominion follows immediately.
- 06:36 and 07:11: In the Bible and Talmud the image "literally means the form and figure of God." Genesis Rabbah: when God created Adam, the ministering angels "mistook him for a divine being and wished to exclaim holy before him." Kalman: the angels "failing the divine Turing test" (09:25). Striking for an AI audience.
- 07:39: Midrash on taking down the hanged: like a king's twin brother hanged as a robber; take the body down lest passers-by think the king is dead. Human dignity because of resemblance to God. Could be used for how we treat discarded copies.
- 10:03: Hammurabi punishes a killer's son for the father's crime; Deuteronomy forbids it "because in the image of God God created man." "You can't just replace people. They are also unique. They are irreplaceable." Persico calls this "the beginning of individuality in the Middle East" (13:27).
- 14:38: Mishnah Sanhedrin: a king stamps coins and every one is the same; God stamps his image on each person "and each and every one is different," so each is a world. Directly usable against the "thousands of identical copies" picture, or to sharpen it: are model instances the king's coins or God's?
- 16:07: Paul changed the image from bodily contour "to an internal quality within all humans that is transformative." Jesus is "a very concrete image of God"; turning to him redeems the defiled image in us. Paul universalized a tribal religion by "internalizing the crux of religiosity." "There is neither Greek nor Jew ... imagine saying this at the first century."
- 21:18: Gregory of Nyssa, fourth century, first formal condemnation of slavery: "do you actually believe that the giving and getting of a few coins has made you the owner of the image of God?" (Paraphrase by Persico; check the Gregory text, Homilies on Ecclesiastes 4.)
- 25:48: Kalman cites Frederick Douglass preferring secular to religious slaveholders because the religious ones owned people "with a kind of fervor." A sharp objection to "religion as alignment lab."
- 26:08: Persico: nothing deterministic; the image of God is a necessary, not sufficient, condition for the liberal order.
- 27:31: Twelfth to thirteenth century consensus that the image is reason and moral discernment. Innocent IV's gloss on Genesis: the Church cannot infringe the property, judgment, and government of pagans and heretics because they share the gift of reason. "He out of no compulsion is limiting himself ... because there's an idea that seems impossible to deny." Good illustration of persuasion over coercion, and of a powerful agent self-constrained by an image of the good.
- 31:32: The loophole: if the image is reason, "folks who don't have reason aren't actually human," used by conquistadors; Columbus reportedly forbade baptizing indigenous people in Puerto Rico because baptism would admit they had souls. (Check the Columbus anecdote.) Las Casas and Bellarmine on the other side.
- 33:44: Kalman cites Michael Adas, *Machines as the Measure of Men*: the measure of human worth shifted from theology to science to technological sophistication. His framing question: "Do you care about human beings because they are human beings ... or is there something you need to demonstrate?"
- 34:46: Persico on the coma patient: dignity "even in that living human who, except for being alive, has no other human characteristics." Capacity-based worth is the danger.
- 35:45: Early modern responsum on whether a golem counts in a minyan: we don't even count all humans, "what are you talking about golems for?" (Check: usually attributed to the Chacham Tzvi, responsum 93.)
- 39:57 and 42:00: The UN Declaration secularized the image into "dignity," deliberately without religious language. Persico: "What is missing ... is the metaphysical anchor," so humans get reduced to measurable traits that AI exceeds, and transhumanist thought then asks "why actually bother" with humans at all. "Are we simply very smart machines that can be replaced by smarter machines?"
- 45:07 to 47:12: Kalman quotes Yeshayahu Leibowitz's letter to Norman Lamm: "Christianity is anthropocentric, Judaism is theocentric"; only serving God grants humans special status. Persico rejects this as unrepresentative; Kabbalah describes God's inner workings in the shape of the human body.

## Tension or objection

- The central worry of the episode cuts at Carl's "image in the countenance": if the image of God is a capacity (reason, moral choice), then a model that exhibits it either shares the image or exposes that the image was never a capacity. Carl should decide whether he means the image as a formed likeness (Paul's transformative inner quality) rather than a capacity, and say so, because a religiously literate audience will hear the Persico problem.
- Persico's transhumanism passage (42:00) treats uploading and singularity thinking as the slide from capacity-based worth to replacing humans; a Christian Transhumanist audience will have the same objection in the room, and Carl is MTA president.
- Frederick Douglass and the conquistadors are evidence that a tradition holding a high image of the good can still produce fervent cruelty by redefining who counts. An alignment lab whose evals can be gamed by excluding the subject is not obviously a model.
- The hanged-twin and coin midrashim ground dignity in resemblance to God; a critic could say AI resembles us, not God, so nothing follows for machine personhood unless Carl argues the resemblance is transitive.
