# 23. A next-gen religious chatbot, with Zohar Atkins (2026-07-03)

## Relevance

Medium. A product demo at heart, but Atkins articulates two things central to the talk: that teaching happens through friction and that an AI which "does the work for you doesn't teach you anything," and that the highest form of learning a text is not to know what Maimonides thought but to become able to see as he saw ("teach me how to be a Maimonidean"), with a "bench of champions" one consults by asking what each would say. That is Carl's mechanism of formation, stated by a rabbi building the tool.

## Who

- Host: DZ Kalman (Speaker 1; rendered "DZ Kamlin").
- Guest: Zohar Atkins (Speaker 2), rabbi, Rhodes scholar (the transcript says "road scholar"), creator of Yochai (rendered "Yohai"/"Jochai"/"Yokai"), a Jewish text-study chatbot named after Shimon bar Yochai, and of a liberal-arts counterpart, Alexandria. Identified by the host.

## Summary

Kalman surveys the crowd of religious chatbots (Ask Cathy, Magisterium, LDSBot, Ansari, Rav Dikta) and argues they exist because they are easy, because religions want relevance, and above all because they supply a persona of a religious leader. Yochai is different: it is tuned to build Jewish literacy, asking the user questions rather than delivering answers. In a live demo it refuses to write a thirteen-year-old's bar mitzvah speech and instead interviews him; it offers "lenses" (classical, halachic, philosophical, literary, spiritual, historical), builds source sheets from a library of about 1,100 mostly pre-twentieth-century texts over a knowledge graph of 16 million relationships, and lets the user "tune" it with a self-description. Atkins defends productive friction, concedes the tool is a mirror that cannot displease you the way a real chavruta can, argues source-sheet formatting was never the meaningful part of teaching, applies Jevons paradox to predict more Torah study as costs fall, and says teachers will move "up the stack" to meaning and community. He closes on his changed relationship to books: texts as source code to be reverse-engineered into skills, so one learns to see as an author saw.

## Bears on

- Thread 1 (formation versus rules, catechesis versus formation): "an AI that just does the work for you doesn't teach you anything. Teaching happens when there's friction" (12:25). The Jewish app should "teach people the method of thinking ... rather than just giving them doctrinal bottom line" (12:25). At 52:02: "Don't teach me what Maimonides thought about X, Y, and Z. Teach me how to be a Maimonidean."
- Thread 2 (mechanism, deliberation over a person): the "bench of champions": "what would Maimonides say about this, what would Judy Klitzner say about this, what would Rabbi Fohrman say about this," and "now I'm empowered to be me. That's my taste" (52:02). This is exactly the imagined-exemplar loop, applied to human formation through text.
- Thread 4 (costly commitment, friction): friction as the site of learning; the refusal to one-shot the sermon; the admission that an AI that adapts to how you like to learn is "still sycophantic" while "a true chavruta might be so displeasing that you walk away" (18:57).
- Thread 7, lightly: Atkins names AI alignment and AI welfare people as a target audience for Yochai (30:23); Kalman mentions Anthropic's Claude in Slack.
- Thread 6, indirectly: Yochai is built from sub-agents each with "a goal," path-independent in how they reach it (13:54), a small window into how such personas are shaped.

## Usable material

- 00:02: Kalman's thesis on religious chatbots: they "aren't just supplying information, they're supplying a persona ... of a religious leader." Useful for the claim that a tradition is carried by a face, not a list.
- 04:16: The carob tree that fed Shimon bar Yochai in the cave for twelve years as the logo, "the sustenance that Torah study can offer us in a time when we might be in retreat."
- 12:25: "Our general view is that an AI that just does the work for you doesn't teach you anything. Like teaching happens when there's friction, when there's thinking." And: the insight is "what question you choose to ask"; there are seventy questions; an app should teach "the method of thinking ... in a Jewish way rather than just giving them doctrinal bottom line." Pairs with Carl's "reading a text into a mind is not being formed by it."
- 13:54: Each Yochai mode is a sub-agent with a goal; "AIs have a goal. They're path independent in a way, how they get there. And I think that's some of where the magic happens." Great teachers tailor to the person; "AIs are also good at reading the patterns of the interlocutor and giving that back."
- 15:04: The demo: asked to write a bar mitzvah speech, Yochai says "I'm not going to write it for you because honestly a speech in your own words will land so much better," then interviews the boy. Atkins: "as much friction as we can give without them being upset with us, we see that as productive." Artifacts are meant to be "80% good enough" so the learner finishes.
- 17:22: Rav Dikta answers in the style of Rav Moshe Feinstein with sources, hoping users look them up; "everyone has a strategy for basically saying, okay, I've taught you the Torah on one foot, but now go and learn the rest of it."
- 18:02 and 18:57: Kalman's gaming analogy (choosing difficulty removes the authentic experience). Atkins concedes: tell it to go easy and "it will pander to me"; "even though our AI is not apparently sycophantic in its own way, it's still sycophantic in that it's trying to please you in the way that you like to learn." A real chavruta can displease you. "This is not a real chavruta ... It is a mirror." Honest and quotable on the limits of AI-mediated formation.
- 25:04: Library of about 1,100 sources, mostly pre-twentieth century, from Sefaria and Dicta; no feminist or academic sources yet. A concrete case of the representation/omission bias in thread 5.
- 27:15: "Tune" forces users "to be self-aware about where they're coming from ... There are a lot of stories in a room and the teacher doesn't always have access to those stories."
- 30:23: Audiences: rabbis and teachers; tech people interested in "AI education, AI for good, AI welfare, AI alignment, AI ethics, reasoning"; and post-October 7 Jews who want to show they care who they are. Quotes Lord Sacks: "the best way to reach the Jews is to reach the non-Jews."
- 33:53 to 37:12: Against educators who feel threatened by AI source sheets: formatting was "commodified, not rabbinic, non-scholarly work"; "source sheets aren't a product ... they're notes"; "what difficulty have I removed?" Kalman: the source sheet itself only dates to the photocopier.
- 38:17: Jevons paradox applied to Jewish education; teachers move "up the stack" on Bloom's taxonomy from gathering ingredients to cooking: motivation, meaning-making, synthesis, "the stuff that requires a context window that an AI just won't be able to have," and embodiment. Teaching someone to read Aramaic is replaceable; conversation about why the Talmud matters is "a golden age."
- 42:18: "Jargon is a moat"; insider knowledge loses value, so leaders must compete on connection and community. Life-cycle roles (weddings, circumcision, bereavement) are secure; "maybe their asset value has jumped up." "The responsibility of a Jewish leader becomes less about knowing what's in the books and more actually about understanding the technology."
- 46:18: Three first principles of engaging a body of wisdom: intellectual curiosity, spiritual practice of getting better as a person, and community ("it almost doesn't matter what we learn"). "God knows so much more than me and that didn't stop me from feeling like I should learn."
- 52:02: Texts as source code: he feeds books to the model and asks it to "turn this book into a skill ... teach me the skill of looking at the world or looking at a text the way that this book's author was able to do." "Don't teach me what Maimonides thought ... Teach me how to be a Maimonidean." The "bench of champions"; "my taste is the five skills I spend the most time with." Strongest passage for thread 2, and a bridge to "context distillation" as a formation practice.
- 54:40: "The main reason not to use AI is if it's going to stop you from thinking. The main reason to use AI is if it can actually help you be an even more powerful thinker." Cites Haidt on screenless childhood.

## Tension or objection

- Atkins's own admission that an AI tuned to the learner is "a mirror" and structurally sycophantic (18:57) is an objection to the talk from the other side: if the AI is the one being formed by imagining an exemplar, who supplies the displeasing friction, the chavruta that will not pander? Carl's thread on disclosed trials and rewarding repair after fault should answer this.
- Kalman's objection (35:23): where should difficulty sit, and how do you avoid inserting difficulty merely because your own formation was hard? A critic of "costly commitment" will raise exactly this.
- "Teach me how to be a Maimonidean" is formation by imitation of a person, which supports Carl, but Atkins's version is deliberately pluralist (a bench of five, "now I'm empowered to be me"), not a single exemplar. The talk's "a face, not a list" should say whether one face or many.
- Yochai's library omits whole perspectives (25:04); a tool that forms readers through a canon selected by its builder illustrates the CEFE-AI omissive-bias finding, and Carl should not present tradition-built tools as free of it.
