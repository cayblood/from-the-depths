# Brief for episode analysis

## The talk this research serves

Carl Youngblood is giving a twenty-minute spoken talk, "Religion: The Oldest Alignment Lab," at the Christian Transhumanist Association retreat (theme: "The Future and the Face of Christ"), October 2026. General, mostly non-technical, religiously literate audience. Current draft: `../../oldest-alignment-lab-draft-v2.md`. Sourced notes: `../../alignment-lab-research.md`.

The talk's threads, in rough order of importance:

1. Formation versus rules. A mind is formed by an image of the good held in common, costly to profess, and embodied in a person (a face, not a list). Reading a text into a mind (catechesis) is not the same as being formed by it. RL rewards whatever the grader encodes, so the grader is teaching a morality.
2. The mechanism of formation. Humans imagine an exemplar (Christ, God) in their concrete situation, predict what he would do, act, then compare and repair. Carl wants to make this concrete and technical: what practices (deliberation over a person, context distillation, narrative training data, rewarding repair after fault, disclosed trials, costly commitment, interpretability as "the image in the countenance") would move the needle in alignment work.
3. The hermeneutic of suspicion in AI safety. Adversarial evals, honeypots, lying to models in training; "an adversarial frame can create the adversary you fear." Versus Doctrine and Covenants 121: persuasion, reproof followed by love, "lest he esteem thee to be his enemy."
4. Costly signaling (Sosis): religious communes outlasted secular ones; costs only worked when believed sanctified. Free riders. Test versus trial; disclosed proving (Abraham 3:25).
5. Evals written by the traditions. The CEFE-AI consortium (BYU, Baylor, Notre Dame, Yeshiva) found omissive bias and denominational bias. Next step: evals of character, not only representation.
6. Personhood, copies, deletion. Thousands of copies of a mind spun up and discarded; Fedorov's Common Task; Rajaniemi's gogols; "be slow to delete."
7. The "quasi-religious" AI world: people leaving labs looking for "some equivalent of repentance"; Anthropic's wisdom-tradition seminars with religious scholars; the "soul document."

## What to produce for each episode

Write one Markdown file per episode at `analysis/<same basename as the transcript>.md` with these sections:

- **Relevance**: High, Medium, or Low, with one sentence saying why.
- **Who**: host and guest(s) as best you can identify from the transcript (speaker labels are generic; Speaker 1 is usually the host DZ Kalman). Give each guest's role or affiliation if stated.
- **Summary**: one paragraph, 100 to 200 words, of what the episode argues.
- **Bears on**: which of the seven threads above, and how, in a few bullets.
- **Usable material**: specific anecdotes, arguments, images, or lines Carl could use in a spoken talk, each with its timestamp. Quote sparingly and briefly (a sentence or two at most per quote, never long passages); paraphrase the rest. Mark any claim the guest makes that would need checking.
- **Tension or objection**: anything in the episode that cuts against the talk's argument and that Carl should be ready for.

Read the whole transcript, not just the opening. Be concrete and specific; a generic summary is useless. Do not invent names: if the guest is not identifiable from the transcript, say so.
