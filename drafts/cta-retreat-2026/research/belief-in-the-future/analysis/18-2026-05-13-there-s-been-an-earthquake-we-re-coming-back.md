# 18. There's Been an Earthquake, We're Coming Back (2026-05-13)

## Relevance

**Low.** A six-minute solo relaunch announcement with no guest and no argument beyond a map of the landscape; its one real value is a third-party observer's confirmation, for thread 7, that Anthropic is making "direct overtures to religious leaders" and that the AI safety world has decided it needs religious communities in its coalition.

## Who

- Host only: DZ Kalman (the transcript has no speaker labels; it is a monologue). No guest.

## Summary

Kalman announces season two after almost a year of hiatus and restates the show's mission. When he started in 2024, the public assumed religion and technology had little to say to each other, so coverage treated the oddity (robot monks, AI Jesuses) as the story, "like a dog that plays chess." That is no longer true, for three reasons he says nobody else has laid out. First, the world's religions have realized in the last year that AI is something to organize around, with denominational working groups and conferences, and with approaches as different as Catholic, Muslim, Jewish, and Latter-day Saint structures. Second, the AI safety world, after years of shouting about danger, has understood that religious wisdom and religious communities must be part of its coalitions if it wants to change culture or pass laws, because religious leaders still carry moral authority and religious communities are where people talk seriously about the good life. Third, and most surprising to him, the AI companies themselves: Anthropic at least is making direct overtures to religious leaders, partly from genuine interest in wielding its new power safely, partly because some developers know their language is already semi-religious. These three shifts are "earthquakes" that have shoved two land masses together. He no longer has to argue that religion and AI belong in conversation; his job is to map the new terrain. The show goes weekly, experiments with video, and affiliates with a faith-and-technology policy network.

## Tension or objection

- **Religion as constituency, not method.** The safety world, as Kalman describes it, wants religious communities for their moral authority and their votes. The talk claims religion has something technical to teach about forming minds. A listener who has absorbed Kalman's framing may hear Carl's claim as overreach: the coalition wants clergy at the press conference, not in the training loop.
- **Reputational reading of the overtures.** Kalman says part of Anthropic's motive is self-awareness about its semi-religious language. A skeptic will say the outreach is image management. Carl should be able to point to something the seminars changed, not just that they happened.
- **Guilt by association.** "Peter Thiel talking about the Antichrist" sits in the same paragraph as Anthropic's overtures. The quasi-religious AI world includes apocalyptic politics as well as wisdom seminars; the talk should not let the one lend credibility to the other.
- **It is not an argument.** Nothing here can be cited as evidence beyond "a podcaster who watches this space says so." Use for framing and corroboration only.
