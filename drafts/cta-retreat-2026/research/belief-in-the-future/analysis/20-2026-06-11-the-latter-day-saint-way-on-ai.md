# 20. The Latter-day Saint way on AI (2026-06-11)

## Relevance

High. An apostle of Carl's own church says on tape that "AI personas need reasons, not rules," that character requires persistence and a moral core formed from "case studies and the examples" of admirable traits, and that "frictions" are the most important part of mortal learning. This is the talk's thesis stated by a Latter-day Saint authority, which gives Carl standing before a Christian audience and a source his own tradition will recognize.

## Who

- Host: DZ Kalman (Speaker 1), who also narrates a long introduction.
- Guest: Elder Gerrit W. Gong (Speaker 2; the transcript renders it "Garrett Gong"), member of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, identified by the host as the man leading the Church's AI response. The episode includes an excerpt of his speech at the Organized Intelligence conference (Salt Lake City, fall 2025) and an interview recorded in Athens at a conference hosted by the American Security Foundation (as the host names it; check).

## Summary

Kalman argues that the Latter-day Saint response to AI is the most holistic of any organized religion because the Church is centralized but small, concentrated enough to drive policy (Utah's 2024 generative-AI law and its mental-health-chatbot amendments), and led at the apostolic level by someone who cares about AI. He reads the Church's internal rules: no AI-generated images of the Father or Christ outside Publishing Services approval, no AI drafting of general conference talks because "AI cannot replace divine inspiration, nor the individual work that invites it," no human, scriptural, or symbolic names (like "Liahona") for Church AI tools. Gong explains the principle behind the rules: AI must not get between a person and God. He grounds the approach in "teach correct principles and let people govern themselves," in protecting and enlarging moral agency, and in the value of "frictions," the costly investments through which mortals learn. He is firm that AI is not and cannot be God, treats the "AI as humanity's child" language as metaphor, and ends by arguing that neither humans nor AI can be constrained into long-term good: AI needs a moral compass and a "gift of possibility," formed by examples of the best human traits, with enough persistence of character for judgment to have a moral core.

## Bears on

- Thread 1 (formation versus rules): Gong at 28:29 states it almost verbatim: constraint cannot produce long-term good "neither in AI nor in people"; guardrails handle catastrophic cases, but character comes from "case studies and the examples, which are the very best, most admirable traits." Also the Joseph Smith line, "teach correct principles and let people govern themselves" (16:39).
- Thread 2 (mechanism of formation): the idea that persona character needs "persistence" so that judgment has a moral core; formation by exemplars; "we learn by doing things, including things that are sometimes challenging."
- Thread 4 (costly signaling, trial): "frictionless relationships" versus the frictions "we invest in because we are concerned and love people," which are "the most important parts of what we're doing" (16:39). The general conference rule rests on the premise that inspiration requires "the individual work that invites it."
- Thread 6 (personhood): Gong concedes AI personas may "have personality" and "react differently depending on how they're treated," but insists on a clear line from human and divine intelligence and refuses anthropomorphizing by name. Cuts both ways for Carl.
- Thread 7 (quasi-religious AI world): Kalman notes Anthropic's language of AI as a child to be raised and as possibly a person; Gong calls it metaphor.
- Thread 5 (evals by traditions), indirectly: the Church writing its own operational rules is a tradition encoding its values into practice.

## Usable material

- 03:58: The Church's rules read aloud by Gong: no AI images of Heavenly Father and Jesus Christ except via Publishing Services and the AI Working Group; general conference speakers asked not to use AI for drafts or final messages "because AI cannot replace divine inspiration, nor the individual work that invites it"; no human, scriptural, doctrinal, or symbolic names such as "Liahona" for Church AI tools. Concrete, memorable, and fitting for a talk that says a mind is formed by work, not by reading in a text.
- 09:32: Gong's use of a midrash on Babel: when a man fell and died no one paid heed, but when a brick fell they wept. He heard it from a rabbi at a conference in Istanbul. He draws from it humility about "the things that we don't know, that we don't know."
- 11:21: Gong grew up in Palo Alto; his father worked with early AI teams and co-authored a book with a Nobel laureate in transistor physics. "We were in Silicon Valley before Silicon Valley became Silicon Valley." (Check the father's co-author; Gong does not name him.)
- 12:13: The Church's concern is theological: how developments "affect the hearts and minds of the children of God," so that people can be "happy and forever." The plan of happiness as human flourishing "for long periods of time."
- 13:57: The core principle: make sure AI "does not get between an individual and their relationship with deity." "AI is math, algorithms are math, and math is not divine." A recipe from a chatbot is fine; feeling in love with one, or in therapy with one, is a chatbot being treated as more than a chatbot.
- 16:39: "Teach correct principles and let people govern themselves"; the task is to find "the timeless elements of those things that seem very timely." Focus on "promotion and protection of individual moral agency and the idea of enlarging capacity, not constricting it." Then the frictions passage: the frictions are "the things that we invest in because we are concerned and love people," and are "the most important parts of what we're doing." Strong for threads 1 and 4.
- 19:43: Gong's four relationships: the Thou (God), the I, the They (society), the It (natural world); AI can distort or accentuate each, "like a black hole" distorting gravity, changing perception. He notes some things' existence now "depends on how it's recorded and the weights in different kinds of AI models."
- 22:12: "AI personas perhaps having personality. They react differently depending on how they're treated and discussed." Then: "God created man, man created AI, AI cannot and will not be God." The second line is a crisp quotable for a Christian audience.
- 23:44: On "AI as humanity's child": AI is developed over time through training "and uses it in sometimes unexpected ways," but it is a metaphor, not a description of the relationship we have with God or our children.
- 25:57: At Mars Hill the same day: the Athenian altar "to the unknown God" amid genuine accomplishment, as a model for keeping humility amid technological achievement.
- 27:38: Robotics will collapse the separation between mind and physicality; the same distinctions apply for AI in or out of a robot.
- 28:12 and 28:29: Kalman quotes Gong's writing: "Can agentic capacity be constrained into promoting the greater good only by do or do not guardrails? AI personas need reasons, not rules." Gong expands: "You cannot constrain someone or an AI into something which is long-term beneficial." Guardrails are for catastrophic harm; beyond that comes "moral compass" and "the gift of possibility," formed from "the case studies and the examples, which are the very best, most admirable traits," with "persistence for the character to have judgment and for the judgment to have a moral core." This is the single most usable passage in the six episodes. (Check: find the published piece Kalman is quoting, likely a Gong essay or address from 2025-2026, for exact wording.)

## Tension or objection

- Gong's anti-anthropomorphism policy (no human names, AI is math, a chatbot "is not more than a chatbot") sits against Carl's thread 6, "be slow to delete," and against any talk that treats models as minds being formed. Carl can note that Gong himself grants personas personality that responds to treatment (22:12), and that the Church's rules are about not confusing AI with God or with human relationships, not about denying that training forms character. Still, a Latter-day Saint listener may hear "form the AI like a disciple" as crossing Gong's line.
- "AI cannot and will not be God" (22:12) is a direct rebuke of god-building and of the more ambitious transhumanist register; Carl, as MTA president, should not be surprised if someone quotes it back.
- Kalman's praise is for the Church's capacity to make and enforce rules (05:13 onward): the Mormon example is admired precisely as a rules-and-policy response, which is the opposite of the talk's "formation, not rules" emphasis. Carl can reconcile: Gong's rules are few, aimed at catastrophic confusion, and sit under a stated principle of self-governance, which matches his own "guardrails for catastrophe, reasons for everything else."
- The Church's reasons for excluding AI from inspired speech rest on inspiration requiring human effort; a skeptic could ask whether Carl's "formation" of AI implies AI could do that work too.
