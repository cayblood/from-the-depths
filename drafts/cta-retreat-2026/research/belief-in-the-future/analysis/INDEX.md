## What the high-relevance episodes add to the talk

- **The mechanism has a precedent.** Witkowski's Talmud point (03) and Schmid's confessional (12) are two concrete instances of "imagine the exemplar and predict what he would do," one as a tradition, one as a deployed model. Muhammad's grading criterion, whether it sounds like his father, is the same move on a model.
- **The thesis has a Latter-day Saint voice.** Gong (20) says reasons not rules, examples not constraints, and frictions as the point of mortality. Carl can quote an apostle rather than argue the point himself.
- **Formation versus rules has a lab-insider witness.** Krieger (11) did the thing the talk recommends, inside Meta, and credits his Talmud years for it even while denying the texts gave him answers.
- **The grader thread has a concrete image.** Lutz's snake-handling scorer (29) is a better illustration than the Psalms experiment, and it comes from inside a lab.
- **"A face, not a list" has its philosopher.** Buber via Atlas (28).
- **Evals by the traditions exist in two forms.** Benchmarks (Wingate, 25) and red-team rubrics (Lutz, 29). Both are about representation and harm, not character; Carl's "evals of character" remains his own proposal.

## Objections to be ready for, by episode

- Texts never changed my mind (Krieger, 11). Answer: the talk claims the practices form the judge, not that the texts contain the answers.
- Catechesis by prompt worked well enough (Schmid, 12). Answer: what the simulation cannot do: no body, no cost, no repair, nobody willing to take responsibility for it.
- Ethics is just more code (Kelly, 17). Answer: the consensus he says is missing is the image held in common, and his own "learn by doing and failing" is formation.
- AI is math, not a mind; don't anthropomorphize (Gong, 20). Answer: his own rules are few, aimed at catastrophic confusion, and sit under self-governance; he grants personas respond to how they're treated.
- The adversarial frame is what got religious rubrics into a lab (Lutz, 29). Answer: distinguish product red-teaming on behalf of users from deceiving the model during training; the church invented the devil's advocate.
- Religion taken seriously is a jailbreak (Lutz, 29). Answer: formation includes judgment about which claims to honor.
- There is no image of the good held in common (Lutz, 29; Kalman passim). The talk assumes one; say what it is.
- Nothing is meeting you on the other side (Atlas, 28). Answer: unresolved, and the talk should admit it.
- Representation is not formation (Wingate, 25). Answer: agreed; that is the gap the talk's asks are meant to close.
