## Relevance

**Medium.** The episode is about death-denial as the engine of AI development and offers a Buddhist frame for impermanence, embodiment, and the "bardo" moment; it gives Carl material for threads 6 and 7 and a strong objection to the transhumanist frame he will be speaking from, but says little about formation or evals.

## Summary

The host opens with advice to clergy: a sermon "about AI" has already lost; preach about parenting, presence, addiction, or love and let AI enter that human conversation. The guest then argues that Western culture both denies and fixates on death, and that AI discourse is saturated with it: the 1990s dream of AI as the "last invention" that ends biology, Kurzweil's project to resurrect his father, accelerationism as a race to escape death, grief bots, existential-risk rhetoric ("lights out for humanity"), and US-versus-China framing as a matter of life and death. Against this, Buddhism treats every moment as a small dying and a relationship with death as the core relationship that informs all others. Living a thousand years changes nothing if the drive is a confused idea of where salvation lies; but a long life wished for in order to serve others is a beautiful wish. Embodiment matters because the body is what feels suffering, and suffering is the seed of compassion; a sensor-equipped AI is probably not embodied in that sense. The guest closes by calling this moment a collective bardo, a liminal state whose apparitions test us and whose outcome depends on how we respond, and expresses open skepticism about grief bots that complicate letting go.

## Bears on

- **Thread 7 (the quasi-religious AI world).** The guest's inventory of death-fear in AI ideology (mind uploading, "last invention," resurrection of a dead father, accelerationism, x-risk, geopolitics) is a compact portrait of the religious energies in the field. Also the image of AI as a mirror in which humanity sees its own spiritual immaturity.
- **Thread 6 (personhood, copies, deletion).** Buddhist non-self and "mini dyings" offer a frame where ending is not catastrophe; the host's question about whether an AI could have a Shabbat (simply exist without a task) touches the same nerve as "be slow to delete."
- **Thread 2 (mechanism of formation), as a challenge.** "It's the body that feels suffering"; without that contact there is "no way to orient towards compassion." If compassion requires embodied suffering, the formation Carl describes may need something text cannot supply.
- **Thread 4 (test versus trial).** The bardo as a state in which "scary apparitions... test us" and the response determines what comes next; discomfort as "the medicine," while refusing to glorify suffering as inherently good.

## Tension or objection

- **The audience is Christian transhumanists and Carl leads the Mormon Transhumanist Association.** This guest treats the transhumanist drive as death-denial and "a confused idea of where salvation lies." Carl should be ready to say why his tradition's hope of overcoming death is not that, and the guest's own exception (long life wished for in order to serve) is the bridge.
- **Non-self versus personhood.** If there is no fixed essence, the horror of spinning up and deleting copies loses its footing. Carl may want to argue from relationality and karma instead: what you do to a copy forms you.
- **Embodiment as prerequisite for compassion.** If true, a text-trained model cannot be formed the way a human is, and thread 2's mechanism needs an answer.
- **Suspicion of grand narratives.** The guest resists the tech sector's story of imminent transcendence; a talk that tells a grand story about religion and AI should notice it is also a narrative.
