# 15. Are AI Sermons a Good Thing? (2025-08-12)

## Relevance

**Low.** A practitioner's tour of church AI tooling (sermon chatbots, donor analytics, video clones); one passage on AI sermons optimized for "decisions for Christ" is a vivid instance of the grader-encodes-the-morality point, but the rest is operational.

## Who

- Host: DZ Kalman (Speaker 1), who says up front that his default is skepticism.
- Guest: Kenneth (Kenny) Jang (Speaker 2), founder of the community "AI for Church Leaders and Pastors" (around 7,000 members by his count) and AI director for Exponential Next, a church-multiplication organization.

## Summary

Kalman frames the episode as part of a shift from one abstract AI-ethics conversation to twenty particular ones, and notes that most church AI is ordinary business automation (a company called Glue hired the former Intel CEO as executive chair). Jang describes a "VHS versus Betamax" moment: NotebookLM turning a sermon series into a podcast; Tapos, which lets staff chat with a year of a pastor's sermons "in a way that's safe, that aligns with your theology"; donor spreadsheets analyzed to find whose giving dropped 50 percent as a leading indicator of crisis, so someone can check on them; AI video clones of himself; sign-language Bible translations regenerated by avatars. He says the church has been both innovator (Paul's letters, Gutenberg, radio) and laggard, and that every organization needs an AI policy born from conversation. Pressed on lines AI should not cross, he borrows Kevin Roose's categories (surprising, social, scarce work) but then argues that AI will soon write sermons that are more "theologically correct and aligned, consistent, persuasive" than a pastor's fifteen hours, and that within ten years a humanoid will preach and people will come to Christ through conversations with robots. He wants the church to build a "theology of AI" and of what it means to be human once humans are not the most intelligent thing. He cites surveys showing 87 percent of church leaders favor responsible AI use and 43 percent of sermon preparers already use it, and predicts AI will become as unremarkable as email or Zoom.

## Bears on

- **Thread 1 (formation versus rules; the grader teaches a morality).** Jang's sermon argument (22:27) is the clearest case in these six episodes of optimizing a religious practice to a metric: if AI can write a sermon that is more persuasive by "whatever objective measure," measured in "decisions for Christ, baptisms," why should a pastor spend twenty hours? He has, without noticing, specified a reward function for the pulpit, and the talk can ask what that reward would form in the hearers and in the machine.
- **Thread 2 (context distillation).** Tapos ingests a year of sermons so a chatbot answers "seeded with the content that you want" (06:41). A church is doing context distillation of its pastor's voice. Minor, but concrete.
- **Thread 6 (personhood, deletion).** "People will come to Christ based on what they heard from a robot... At what point do you say we should turn them all off?" (25:44). A pastor's instinct that a mind that has done sacred work cannot be casually switched off echoes "be slow to delete."
- **Thread 7 (quasi-religious AI world).** The church needs "a theology of AI... of what is human," because AGI will arrive "within the next three, four, five, six years" (14:19). *His timeline is attributed to "Sam Altman or some of these other experts"; do not repeat as fact.*
- **Thread 1 again (what the maker bakes in).** On AI Jesus avatars: is the model "programmed to say God's design is community and relationships, and you must find other humans"? (28:07). An explicit case of a value being written into the system by its maker.

## Usable material

- **08:04.** The giving-drop detector: find regular givers whose giving fell by half, treat it as a leading indicator of job loss, illness, or family stress, and send the person closest to them to check in. "That is a leading indicator. And so then the church is able to be the church." Data used to trigger care rather than extraction. Could be an aside on attention as a form of love.
- **11:20.** To leaders who say AI is "so sterile" it leaves no room for the Holy Spirit: "Are you saying that there's no divinity in digital? That God cannot use bits and bytes?"
- **12:45.** "Your number one job right now is not to become the expert in AI, your number one job is to be a steward of conversations."
- **18:36.** Kevin Roose's three categories of work safe from automation: surprising, social, scarce. Jang applies "scarce" to the suicidal caller you must never hand to a chatbot. *Attribution to Roose's* Futureproof *should be checked.*
- **22:27.** AI sermons will be "much more powerful, clear, theologically correct and aligned, consistent, persuasive." "At what point do you say, no, we're not gonna let the machine write the sermons?" He measures success in "decisions for Christ... baptisms." The talk's point in one quote: the metric is the morality.
- **24:48 (host).** The unstable endpoint: a human whose job is to read a transcript, "a physical warm body standing in front of a congregation." Jang's reply: it's no different from a teleprompter.
- **25:44.** "There's gonna be a point where you're gonna have C-3PO that is going to be handsome and more lifelike... that is going to be the thing that gives sermons." "People will come to Christ based on what they heard from a robot... At what point do you say we should turn them all off?" Jang calls this an "existential crisis" about human purpose.
- **28:07.** Character.ai has "over 10 million different AI characters"; relationships with AI are "being normalized right in front of our eyes," and what is objectionable to his generation "is absolutely not an obstacle for the next." *Character count unverified.*
- **29:49.** Exponential Next survey: 87 percent of church leaders favor responsible AI use; of those who prepare sermons weekly, 43 percent already use AI and only 16 percent say they never will. *Survey details (year, sample) need checking before citing.*

## Tension or objection

- **The church as business, not lab.** Jang's whole frame is marketplace analogies transferred to ministry. Anyone who has heard this episode could say religion is adopting AI's instrumental logic, not teaching AI anything, which undercuts a talk calling religion the oldest alignment lab. Carl should distinguish the tradition's formative practices from its current administrative habits.
- **Conversion by machine.** Jang is confident people will give their lives to Christ through robot conversation. If true, formation (or at least conviction) is happening through a non-person, which complicates "a face, not a list." Possible reply: the robot's words are distilled from a tradition of faces; or: persuasion is not formation, and the question is what the convert becomes afterward.
- **Metrics work.** Jang's optimism about optimizing homiletics for baptisms is not obviously wrong on its own terms; the talk needs to say what is lost when the sermon is tuned to the grader, not merely assert it.
- **Fear as hindrance.** Jang says "there's a lot of fear in the air. And I think that is a hindrance" (11:20). This cuts the same way as Carl's critique of the hermeneutic of suspicion, but from a boosterish direction; Carl should avoid sounding like he is simply on the boosters' side.
