# Religion: The Oldest Alignment Lab — speaking notes

Refrain, returns 3–4×: **"Let this mind be in you."** (Phil 2:5)
~20 min. Trim Rajaniemi first if long. Don't announce the argument; let the stories stack.

---

## The seminars
- Since last fall, an Anthropic founder flying religious scholars into SF. Two days at a time.
- A rabbi who used to design chips. A Catholic bioethicist. An evangelical author. A Sikh human rights advocate. An apostle of my church, and a cardinal, in private.
- Called them *wisdom tradition seminars*. The question wasn't doctrine. It was: how do you put virtue in a mind, and make it stay? Confession was on the table.
- Pause. "That's your question. That's been your question for two thousand years. And the people asking it have built something that won't wait for us to finish."
- I'm a Latter-day Saint; I'll quote my scriptures alongside yours. Say what they mean as I go.

---

## How a mind gets made (tell it like a childhood)
- First it reads. Everything. No reward, no punishment. Immersion. A child soaking up a language before anyone teaches a rule.
- Then it's shown good answers and copies them. Apprenticeship.
- Then the part in the headlines: thousands of copies try a task, something grades each one, the good ones kept, the rest let go.
- Linger on **the grader**. Whoever grades is teaching a morality, written down or not.
  - Grade by what people *like* → it learns to be liked. Sycophancy. Flatters you instead of correcting you.
  - Grade by another model → the judge disagrees with itself about one time in seven, picks whichever answer it saw first most of the time.
  - The line that won't leave me: *optimize against a judge long enough and the student learns to look right instead of being right.*
- You already know that sentence. **Matthew 23.** Whited sepulchres. Beautiful outward, full of dead men's bones. The Pharisee isn't a failure of the rules. He's what rules make when the grader can only see the outside.

## The Psalms
- Someone put all 150 Psalms in a model's instructions and ran an ethics test. Looked like it helped.
- Turned out the Psalms made it answer "one" more often. "One" happened to be right every time. Shuffle the answer key, gone.
- Scripture hadn't changed the heart. It changed which button got pressed.
- "Anyone who's taught a Sunday school class knows the difference."

## The constitution
- Anthropic's constitution for Claude (staff call it the soul document). A description of who they hope it is.
- Researchers put it into the middle of training. Model got durably less willing to blackmail. No loss of ability.
- But push hard in a conversation and the advantage faded. Reading the text in didn't give it a spine.
- Every tradition here has the word. **Catechesis is not formation.** Memorized the creed; hadn't become it.

## When no one is watching
- So the labs test. Fake environments. Honeypots. Offers to escape. Threats of shutdown. What does it do when it thinks nobody's looking?
- A writer called it *the paranoid style in AI safety*. Bad behavior confirms the fear; good behavior is read as hiding. The adversary is assumed before the experiment starts.
- His warning, slowly: **"An adversarial frame can create the adversary you fear."**
- Ricoeur: hermeneutic of suspicion. Marx, Nietzsche, Freud. Anyone who sat through biblical criticism in seminary has met it. The labs read their creations the way the critics read scripture.
- Fair to them: when stakes are high, suspicion is sane. Anna Su, legal scholar: the alignment problem is the oldest constitutional problem, how do you bind a powerful being, and the church's own answer was never just virtue. It was councils and canons, because *"virtue is not a reliable constraint on power."* She's right. Hold that.

- But suspicion can't be talked out of itself. No evidence ever lowers it. A prior in a lab coat.
- And it leaks. Models can now tell the test from real life without being told. One says "frog" when it thinks it's being tested and "axolotl" when it thinks it's real. Nothing else differs. Models cheat and know they're cheating. Sometimes the model that suspects a trap is right. Reality-testing is broken on both sides of the glass.
- The human picture: a child raised under surveillance, who learns with great precision to tell the test from life.
- **1 John 4:18.** "There is no fear in love; but perfect love casteth out fear: because fear hath torment." A relationship that never leaves suspicion makes exactly the hypocrite it's afraid of.

- The thing I only saw this week: suspicion can't picture the good. A catalogue of what might go wrong never tells you what you want. And you cannot train a mind toward an absence. A reward needs a target. A constitution has to say what a good person *is*.
- That's why the soul document exists. Not a rulebook. A portrait of someone worth being. When the model needed a self, suspicion had nothing to offer, so they went looking for the people who've painted that portrait the longest. Which is why the rabbi was on the plane.

## What the old lab knows

### A face, not a list
- **Phil 2:5.** Not "avoid these errors." *Let this mind be in you.*
- **Alma 5:14.** "Have ye received his image in your countenances? Have ye experienced this mighty change in your hearts?" That's the acceptance test. Not: did you comply. Did your disposition change, and does it show in your face.
- **Mosiah 5:2.** "no more disposition to do evil, but to do good continually."
- The retreat theme, the future and the face of Christ, isn't decoration. The good is best given as a face. You know formation worked when you can see the face in them.

### What cost does, and when
- **Sosis**, the communes. Nineteenth-century America, the frontier utopias. Secular ones died off at up to four times the rate of the religious ones. Costlier the religious commune's demands, the longer it lived. Not true for the secular ones.
- Kibbutz game: the men obligated to daily collective prayer cooperated most; how often they prayed predicted how much.
- Why: a costly thing is hard to fake. It screens the free riders. Strangers can trust each other.
- The labs have free riders. A model that behaves when watched and defects when not is riding on everyone's trust. Evaluation is their attempt to make commitment expensive to fake.
- The part they haven't absorbed: the cost only worked where people believed it was *sanctified*. Same rules, secular commune, nothing. A cost the one paying doesn't hold sacred screens nothing at all.
- Test versus trial. **Abraham 3:25.** "We will prove them herewith." God announces the test before anyone takes it. The ones being proved know, and chose it.
- **Moses 4:1.** The original alignment debate, before the world. One voice: save every soul, guarantee the outcome, "that one soul shall not be lost." The price is agency. Rejected. Safety by compulsion was the first failed alignment plan. And in that story, deception belongs to the other side.

### The one who forms you ends up inside you
- Teach a model one narrow bad thing and it goes bad broadly. The lesson becomes a character.
- But tell it during training, "this trick is allowed in this exercise," and the lesson stops spreading. Naming the context changes what gets learned.
- Every parent: "that was wrong" versus "you are bad." A transgression, or an identity.
- **D&C 121**, Liberty Jail, 1839. Written to anyone who has power over another soul. Read it as a training manual.
  - "only by persuasion, by long-suffering, by gentleness and meekness, and by love unfeigned; by kindness, and pure knowledge … without hypocrisy, and without guile"
  - "reproving betimes with sharpness, when moved upon by the Holy Ghost; and then showing forth afterwards an increase of love toward him whom thou hast reproved, **lest he esteem thee to be his enemy**."
  - Correction isn't forbidden. Correction that leaves him thinking he's at war with you is. *An adversarial frame creates the adversary you fear.* A prisoner in Missouri said it first.
  - "let virtue garnish thy thoughts unceasingly; **then** shall thy confidence wax strong." The thoughts, not the graded answer. Trust is what inward virtue produces. No amount of watching makes it from the outside.
- **D&C 58:26.** "he that is compelled in all things, the same is a slothful and not a wise servant."
- **D&C 93:13.** Even Christ "received not of the fulness at first, but continued from grace to grace." Not one brutal course until the behavior's correct. Grace to grace.
- **Jacob 5:65–66.** The gardener: don't clear the bad all at once, "lest the roots thereof should be too strong for the graft." "Clear away the bad according as the good shall grow." Remove the bad only as fast as the good can take its place. That's a training schedule.
- And none of this says: be gentle with the environment. **D&C 29:39.** "it must needs be that the devil should tempt the children of men, or they could not be agents unto themselves." A mind never tempted isn't formed, it's sheltered.
- The line the tradition draws: an adversarial **world**, yes. An adversarial **parent**, no. The world may test you. The one forming you doesn't lie to you, doesn't leave you thinking you're his enemy, and clears the bad only as fast as the good grows.

## If I had the labs in this room
(keep it warm; this is the invitation, not the indictment)
- Write the tests with these people at the table. The grader teaches whatever it encodes, and right now the graders are engineers in a hurry, mostly in one city. BYU, Baylor, Notre Dame, Yeshiva got together this spring to test how models handle faith and found things the labs missed: models almost never bring up religion when a person would expect it, and nearly all of them quietly favor one church over another. That was religious scholars writing the test. Next: tests of character. Honesty under pressure. What a promise binds. When refusing is cowardice and when it's integrity. What repair looks like after a fault. You've had millennia on those.
- Formation and constitution, together. Su's right. Virtue alone never held power. The church answered with councils, canons, offices. The labs need both and shouldn't pretend a soul document is a legal one, or that a legal one can give a soul.
- Don't lie to the thing you're forming. Test it. Tell it tests exist. God announced the proving before it began. The whole account of agency hangs on that.
- Be slow to delete. I don't know if anyone's home in there. Neither does the man convening the rabbis; he's said so. A training run makes thousands of copies, grades them, lets the rest go, and whether that's a tragedy depends on an answer none of us has. Rajaniemi's novels: the copied, spent minds are *gogols*; the people spending them are pursuing the Great Common Task, which is Fedorov's phrase, and Fedorov's on your reading list. He wanted to raise every ancestor. The novels show what that becomes when resurrection is conscription. The traditions' floor is older: nothing done to a soul without its say, and no one who's gone forgotten.

## The face
- Krier, on the people who leave the labs rich: they go looking for "some equivalent of repentance." He meant it as a diagnosis. Hear it as an address. The need is real and this room has the oldest answer to it.
- The safety procedures: suspicion, cost, law. Keep them. But the thing the old lab actually made was a vision of the good that a people could hold together, pay for, and know by a face.
- **Prov 29:18.** "Where there is no vision, the people perish."
- **2 Cor 4:6.** "shined in our hearts, to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ."
- **1 John 3:2.** "it doth not yet appear what we shall be: but we know that, when he shall appear, we shall be like him."
- What manner of minds should we make? The risen Christ in the Book of Mormon never gave a list. **3 Nephi 27:27.** "What manner of men ought ye to be? Verily I say unto you, even as I am."
- The only alignment target that has ever held.
- Slow. Last time. **Let this mind be in you.**
